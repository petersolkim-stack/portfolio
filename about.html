About me

My Story

A little about who I am, what drives me and how I got here.

“Good marketing starts the same way good design does: by paying attention to people.”

Where my passion began

I grew up behind the counter of my family’s laundry business, Easy Wash and Fold in Hyattsville. Between balancing cash drawers and helping customers with drop-off and pick-up, I learned early that small details, like a friendly greeting or an order that’s always on time, are what turn first-time customers into regulars.

In 2022 I started CeesBeads, designing and handcrafting products and selling them through my own storefront. Building that brand from scratch, from product photos and listings to packaging and TikTok videos, is when I realized how much I love the place where business and design meet. That led me to study marketing and management at the University of Maryland’s Robert H. Smith School of Business.

How I fuel it

CeesBeads is still my creative playground. I’ve filmed, edited and published more than 100 videos and grown an audience of over 30,000 followers on TikTok and Instagram by testing content, watching the data and adapting quickly to trends.

On campus, I’m Vice President of Design for the American Marketing Association, where I lead a team of three designers and our organization’s rebrand. I’m also a Media, Self & Society Scholar, which keeps me thinking about how media shapes the way people see brands and themselves.

How I’ve built my skills

I got a head start through Montgomery College’s dual enrollment program, earning an Associate of Arts in Business in 2025 before starting at Smith, where I hold a 4.0 GPA.

Each internship has added a new layer. At Flowerlane I designed newsletter, sales and social assets in Canva and wrote the copy. At RedBlack Consulting I applied design thinking and strategic analysis to real client problems and presented the results in reports and slide decks. Now at Joe’s Movement Emporium I support branding, outreach research and data systems for a community arts nonprofit. Along the way I’ve built my toolkit in Photoshop, InDesign, Premiere Pro, Tableau and Qualtrics.

Design thinking, applied to business

I approach marketing problems as design problems. I start with empathy, researching users, competitors and industry trends to understand what people actually need. Then I define the real problem, ideate options, prototype quickly (a post, a newsletter layout, a slide) and test it against engagement data before scaling up.

Every design decision should connect back to a business goal, whether that’s brand awareness, customer acquisition, retention or conversion. A consistent visual identity builds brand equity, and a clear user experience lowers friction in the customer journey. Pairing creative work with that strategic lens is what I bring to a team.
